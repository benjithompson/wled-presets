# WLED Presets

A web page where anyone on your network can control all your WLED lights at once, and an admin console to set it up.

- **Public page** — tap a **scene** to play a preset on every device, or pick a solid color. Below the scenes, the **games** have their own group of buttons the same size: one tap opens the game on the Play page and starts it. Live status shows which lights are online. Light and dark themes. Works well on phones and can be added to the home screen.
- **Admin console** (`/admin`)
  - **Scenes** — create, edit, reorder, test, export and import. For each device, a scene picks either one of its **WLED device presets** or one of the **built-in effects** (drawn by the server on all that device's lights, with its colors, speed and brightness under **Colors, speed…**), with no trip to the layout editor. Built-in effects need the device's lights in the layout. "Quick fill" sets every device to a preset with the same name, or every device to the same built-in effect. For effects on single objects or segments, use the layout editor (see below; **Effects per object** on the Scenes page): an object's own effect wins over its device's.
  - **Effects** — thirty-three effects that the server draws for each LED, 60 times a second, in floating-point color, and streams to the lights. Thirteen are **basics**, the standard ones you would expect: Solid color (one color, or the string split into equal parts), Gradient, Color blocks (red, green, red, green…), Rainbow, Color fade, Breathe, Blink, Strobe, Theater chase, Color wipe, Twinkle, Sparkle and Bouncing dot; with **My colors** they play exactly the colors you pick, and controls that do nothing for an effect (the speed of a solid color) are hidden. Ten run along one object's LEDs as a string (1-D), from its first point to its last (or back), skipping off segments: Aurora, Fireworks (rockets, bursts and sparks with drag and crackle), Inferno, Meteor shower, Ocean (waves of several lengths with foam on the crests), Plasma, Starlight (with shooting stars), Lightning storm, Raindrops (ripples that spread and cross) and Candy cane. Ten are 3-D: they light each LED by where it is in the yard, so they fill a bush, spiral up a tree, or play over the whole yard as one picture: Color wash, Laser scan, Bonfire (fire that climbs from the ground), Vortex, Shockwaves, Nebula, Snowfall (flakes that leave a glow where they touch), Fireflies, Starburst (3-D fireworks) and Equalizer. 3-D effects have a **Direction** (left to right, upward, toward the street, from the middle out…). With nothing selected in the layout editor, **Whole yard effect** gives every object an effect at once (a 3-D one as one picture over the yard, a 1-D one along each object); objects and segments with their own effect play theirs instead. A single segment of a roof line or other line can play its own effect: click the segment, then pick one in its **Segment effect** section; the line's effect fills the rest. To give several objects and segments one effect, Ctrl/⌘-click them (in the views, the object list or the 3D view; Shift-click in the list picks a range; Esc clears): the inspector then edits them together, and a setting you change goes to each of them. In the layout editor, pick an object's effect, colors (16 palettes or your own), speed, intensity, size and brightness in its **Effect** section; **Play effects** (P) animates them in every view and, with Lights on, on the real lights. **Scenes ▾** saves them as a scene for the public page, or opens a scene to change it (the Scenes page links there too). A playing scene keeps running with nobody watching, shows in the Play page's simulator, pauses for a game and comes back after it, and plays again after a restart. Choosing another scene or a color stops it.
  - **Sequences** — an editor for animated light shows, built like a video editor (Admin → Sequences). A preview of the yard (front or 3D) plays exactly what the lights get, with a **Now playing** list of every clip and transition at the playhead; point at an object to see what lights it. Below it, a timeline with a ruler, a playhead, markers and **layers**: drag an effect from the browser onto a layer (or double-click a layer) to add a **clip**, which plays that effect on the objects, segments or whole yard you picked (in the Objects tab or by clicking the preview). Drag clips to move them (also to another layer; Alt-drag copies), drag their edges to trim, and drag the knobs at their top corners to fade in and out. Upper layers cover lower ones, mixed by each layer's blend mode (Normal, Add, Lighten, Multiply) and opacity; layers can be hidden, solo or locked, and reordered by their grip. Two clips that overlap on a layer **cross over**: one effect into the next, or one object to another; drop a **transition** on a clip to choose how (Crossfade, Dip to black, Wipe in four directions, Iris, Sparkle dissolve). **Audio tracks** take MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A or FLAC files (uploaded to the server, shown as waveforms, trimmable) and play in the browser in step with the lights; set a tempo to see beats and snap to them, or press M while it plays to tap markers. Snapping (clip edges, markers, the playhead, beats), zoom (Ctrl/⌘ + wheel), a box to select, split (S), copy and paste, duplicate, undo and Ctrl/⌘+S work as in other editors. **Lights** plays it on the real lights as you edit. **Use in a scene** makes a scene that plays the sequence (it loops) for the public page; a scene's editor can also pick a sequence.
  - **Devices** — add by address or scan the network. The presets stored on a device are imported when you add it, and again when its address changes; **Import** reads them again at any time. Play a preset to test it, and enable or disable a device.
  - **Layout** — place every light object where it really is, in 3D: rooflines, other lines of lights (windows, doors, a fence, a path), bushes and trees (a spiral, or strands that zigzag). The front view (over a picture of the house) moves objects across and up, the top view across and toward the street, and the 3D view (three.js, loaded only when opened) shows the whole yard with glowing LEDs; click an LED there to select its object. A measure tool sets the real scale (feet or meters), a ground line sets height 0, and the side panel takes exact positions. The editor is a CAD-style workspace that fills the window: a tool palette (V select, A edit points, C split, H pan, R roofline, L line, B bush, T tree, M measure), one to three viewports (each one shows the front, top or 3D view and can be made big), an object list in which the selected object opens to show its properties right under it, and a status bar with the pointer position and the Snap, Ortho, Grid and Grid snap switches. The grid size next to them opens the grid settings: the size and its units (feet, inches, meters or centimeters, whatever the units of the layout), quick sizes, and how often a brighter line comes; these are remembered in the browser. The wheel zooms at the pointer, the middle button or Space-drag pans, F zooms to the selection, Home zooms to fit. Lines snap to points, midpoints, lines and alignments, and to the grid with Grid snap on (Alt holds snapping off); Shift keeps 45° steps while drawing, and Shift-drag moves a point or an object straight across or straight up and down from where it was (for example, to raise the middle of a roofline into a peak); a click on the end of a line continues it; Ctrl/⌘+Z undoes; Ctrl/⌘+S saves. **File → Export the layout** downloads it as one JSON file (as it is in the editor, unsaved changes too) with its pictures, the WLED devices its objects use and the game area; **File → Import a layout** puts such a file (or a bare layout) in place of the saved layout and its pictures, after asking. Objects keep their device when it is on this install (the same id, address or name); others are left without one. LEDs spread along the real 3D length of a line, so a roofline that runs back along the side of the house gets its share. Each object has the number of LEDs in it. Games and the simulator use this map. Each object has a WLED device and the LED numbers at its first and last point; LEDs between objects (gaps, a hidden side of the house) stay off. A roof line can be split at a corner, and a corner can be pinned to its real LED number. **Match to the lights** (in the line's **LEDs** section) does that for the whole line: draw its rough shape, then the wizard walks the start, each corner and the end, and one LED blinks red on the house; move it to the real spot (buttons, a slider, or the arrow keys, Shift for 10 at a time) and press **Found it** (Enter). The stretch from the point before up to the blinking LED glows, on the house and in the drawing, so it is easy to follow along the roof. The red LED never goes past the last LED the device has in WLED (its LED count, as set in WLED's LED preferences); at the end, **Last LED in WLED** jumps there for a line that ends the chain. When the line's lights come in strands (50 bulbs each, for example), the wizard says which strand and bulb the red LED is on, counted from LED 0 of the device, and Page Up/Page Down jump from one strand joint to the next. The start and the end can move past the line's LEDs, so the line also learns its real number of LEDs; a corner that lands past later corners unpins them, to be matched next. Typing the LED numbers of a line, a corner or a segment by hand is still there, folded under **Advanced**. Click one straight segment of a roof line to light up only that segment, set its start and end LED (segments never share an LED: when one segment ends at LED 49, the next starts at 50), or turn it off (skipped: for a stretch that only links two lit segments; its LEDs stay dark and games skip it). While you edit, the selected object glows blue on the simulator and the real lights (first LED green, last LED red, corners amber), and each change to its LED numbers shows at once through DDP over UDP (port 4048). The lights go back to normal about 2 seconds after you leave the editor.
  - **Light strings** — tell the editor what each object is made of: a light string or strip with its chip, voltage, LED spacing, LEDs per strand and current per LED. Common ones are built in (including NovaBright 12 V WS2811 bullet pixels at 4 in, 50 per strand), and you can add your own. The editor then shows a line's drawn length against the length of its string, offers **Use N LEDs** to fit the LEDs to the line, marks where each new strand starts (with its LED number), and estimates the power. A single segment shows how many LEDs fit it at that spacing; **Use N LEDs here** gives it that many and moves the LEDs after it along, as on the real string. Pick a light string for **New objects** (Yard panel) and lines you draw get the right number of LEDs, with each segment's LED count shown while you draw.
  - **Custom objects** — the object designer (O, or **New custom object**) draws how a string is laid out on your own object: a star, a wreath, an arch, a window, a candy cane, or anything else, from a template or point by point. A design can have several strokes (the wire jumps between them without LEDs), closed or open; the LEDs spread along the wire at the string's spacing and keep fitting it as you change the drawing. LED 0, the last LED and each new strand are marked. Designs go to a library: place one in the yard as often as you like, scale it, turn it and face it toward the street or to the side. Changing a design changes every copy.
  - **Pictures to trace over** — **Pictures ▾** in the layout editor takes a picture of the front of the house for the front view (a photo, or a Street View screenshot taken straight on) and a picture of the yard from above for the top view (a satellite view screenshot, for example from Google Maps, or a drone photo). The picture from above also lies on the ground in the 3D view. To line it up, **Align** asks for a spot on the picture (a front corner of the house), then where that spot really is (the end of the roofline; it snaps), then a second pair: the picture moves, turns and resizes to fit both (Enter after the first pair only moves it). Or measure something on it in the top view and type its real length, then **Scale the picture**. Its width, turn, position and strength (0 hides it) are in the inspector when nothing is selected. Then draw bushes and trees over it where they really are. Only signed-in users can load the picture from above, as it shows where the house is.
  - **Real lights** — a running game also goes to each object's WLED device; when the game ends (or the last Play page closes), each device goes straight back to its color, scene or preset. On the Play page, a signed-in admin can open **Debug** to turn this off or limit the brightness. The game goes to each object's WLED device (up to 60 frames per second, DDP over UDP: long strips go in several packets and show as one frame), with a brightness limit. Objects with no device stay in the simulator. Nothing is sent while no game runs. If DDP does not reach a device (for example, a router between two networks drops it), the app falls back to the WLED JSON API over HTTP at about 12 frames per second, and says so in the debug panel and the Activity log.
  - **Games** — which lights the games use. Games play the roof as it looks from the street, measured in the front view rather than in LEDs: a comet moving at a steady speed moves evenly across the front of the house, a stretch at an angle to the street is crossed faster (it looks shorter), and where the roof runs back along the side of the house or behind another part of the roof, so it cannot be seen from the street, games still light it but run through it very quickly (at most six times as many LEDs a second as along the front, so it shows as a quick streak and not one flash), with nothing to leave out (the picture shows these lights gray-blue, as **Not seen from the street**). This uses the depth of each point in the layout. Leave out other lights that cannot be seen (behind a tree): a whole object, single segments of a roof line or other line, or a number of LEDs at either end. A picture of the yard shows each part in the color of its job in games (roof, bushes, trees, the rest) and what is left out in red; point to an object or segment to find it, and **Show on the lights** shows the same picture on the house. Scenes and effects still use every light; lights left out stay dark during a game.
  - **Activity** — live log of what was applied, by whom, and every admin change.
  - **Settings** — page title and subtitle, color picker on or off, default scene and auto-revert time, discovery subnets, admin password.

- **Play** (`/play`) — games that use the lights on the house, controlled from a phone with one big button (tap it, or hold it down). Every game uses the whole yard, and each part has the same job in every game: the **roof** is where the game is played; **bushes** hold what you have won; **trees** are meters that fill from the base up; **windows, doors and other lines** (and custom objects) are the house reacting: they catch the color of every explosion and glow warmer with a combo. Gold always means points, red a mistake. A yard without bushes, trees or windows still plays on the roof alone.
  - **Comet Catcher** — stop the comet inside the green gate on the roof; each catch lights a bush.
  - **Cascade** — orbs of every color roll along the roof under real gravity (they speed up down its slopes and bounce off each other). One tap sets off a blast where the white spark is; every orb it touches bursts and sets off its neighbours. Pop enough in one chain reaction to clear the level. Each popped orb drips its color off the gutter, past the windows, into the bush below; the trees fill like a thermometer. Later levels bring more orbs, smaller blasts, a gold orb (worth more, bigger blast) and armored orbs that need two blasts.
  - **Overflow** — hold the button to pour light from the gutter into each bush and tree in turn; let go so it settles inside the white ring. The pour speeds up the longer you hold, and the light keeps rising for a moment after you let go. Its color says how close you are: blue when far below, green and yellow nearer, gold in the ring, red over it. A stop in the ring fires a rocket up to the roof (dead center: PERFECT, a burst in every color and a shockwave over the house); successes in a row multiply the points. Later levels: a thinner ring, a faster pour, then a ring that moves.
  - **Roof Duel** — Fire (the left end of the roof) against Ice (the right end), on two phones; each phone turns its player's color. Tap when the ball is in your end zone to send it back: the later the hit, the faster it flies (a hit at the tip is a SMASH). The ball slows climbing to a peak and speeds up coming down. The roof is split between the teams and the line moves toward whoever is losing; each point flies into the scorer's bushes, the trees heat up with a long rally, and each side's windows glow in its team's color. First to 5. Alone, tap twice to play the house.

A *scene* is what v1 called a *public preset*. A *WLED preset* is a preset stored on one device.

## Run with Docker

```bash
docker run -d \
  --name wled-presets \
  --network host \
  -e ADMIN_USERNAME=admin \
  -e ADMIN_PASSWORD=change-me-to-something-long \
  -e DISCOVERY_ON_STARTUP=1 \
  -e DISCOVERY_AUTO_SAVE=1 \
  -v /path/to/appdata:/app/data \
  ghcr.io/benjithompson/wled-presets:latest
```

Or use [docker-compose.yml](docker-compose.yml). Then open `http://<host>:8790`.

Host networking lets discovery scan your LAN. Without it, add devices by IP address.

### Environment variables

| Variable | Default | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `ADMIN_USERNAME` / `ADMIN_PASSWORD` | — | Creates the first admin account when none exists. Ignored after that. |
| `PORT` | `8790` | Web port. |
| `DATABASE_PATH` | `/app/data/wled-presets.sqlite` | SQLite file. |
| `PUID` / `PGID` | `99` / `100` | Owner of the data folder. The server runs as this user. |
| `DISCOVERY_ON_STARTUP` | `0` | Scan for devices when the container starts. |
| `DISCOVERY_AUTO_SAVE` | `0` | Add devices found by the startup scan. |
| `DISCOVERY_SUBNETS` | auto | Comma-separated CIDR ranges to scan. Overrides the subnets in Settings. |
| `DISCOVERY_DEBUG` | `0` | Log discovery details. |
| `DISCOVERY_HOSTS` | — | Extra hosts to probe on every scan, comma-separated. |
| `DISCOVERY_PORTS` | `80` | Ports to probe on each address, for example `80,8080` or `9101-9104`. |
| `STATUS_INTERVAL_MS` | `10000` | How often device status is checked. |
| `SECURE_COOKIE` | `0` | Set to `1` when you reach the app only over HTTPS. |
| `TRUST_PROXY` | `0` | Set to `1` behind a reverse proxy, so logs show the real client IP. |

With no admin account and no environment variables, open `/admin` from the server itself to create one.

## Upgrade from v1

Point v2 at the same data folder. It reads the v1 database as it is: devices, public presets (now scenes), imported presets, the default preset and the auto-revert time all carry over. The public page keeps its "Christmas Lights" title; change it in Settings.

What changes:

- Admins sign in once more, because sessions are now stored in the main database. `SESSION_SECRET` is no longer used.
- Cookies are no longer `Secure` by default. Set `SECURE_COOKIE=1` if you use HTTPS.
- v1 backup files still import. v2 backups also store device addresses, so a backup restores onto a new install.

## Development

Requires Node.js 24 or newer.

To start everything with one command:

```bash
./run.sh
```

The script installs dependencies and creates `.env` when they are missing. Then it starts four fake WLED devices, the API, and the web client. It also sets `DISCOVERY_HOSTS` and `DISCOVERY_PORTS`, so **Discover** finds the fake devices. Open http://localhost:5790. Press Ctrl+C to stop all three.

To run the parts one at a time:

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `npm run dev:all` | Same as `./run.sh`, without the setup steps. |
| `npm run mock` | Starts four fake WLED devices on 127.0.0.1:9101–9104. Run it in its own terminal. |
| `npm run dev` | Starts the API on port 8790 and the web client with hot reload on port 5790. Open http://localhost:5790. Set `WEB_PORT` or `PORT` in `.env` to change the ports. |
| `npm run check` | Runs the typecheck, the tests and the production build. |

| Part | Stack | Folder |
|---|---|---|
| Server | Node.js 24 (runs TypeScript directly), Hono, better-sqlite3, Zod | [server/src](server/src) |
| Web client | React 19, Vite, Tailwind CSS 4, TanStack Query, React Router | [web/src](web/src) |
| Shared types | TypeScript | [shared/types.ts](shared/types.ts) |
| Tests | Vitest, against fake WLED devices | [server/test](server/test) |

Live updates use server-sent events: the server checks every device on a timer and pushes changes to open pages.

Frames reach the lights through one pipeline ([server/src/framePipeline.ts](server/src/framePipeline.ts)). Everything that draws is a source: the game host (games and scenes, [server/src/game/host.ts](server/src/game/host.ts)) and the editors' preview ([server/src/layoutPreview.ts](server/src/layoutPreview.ts)). One clock ticks every running source 60 times a second, on a steady beat, and the frame of the most important source goes out: an editor over a game, a game over a scene. The pipeline maps each frame to the devices' LED numbers and hands it to the sender ([server/src/lightSender.ts](server/src/lightSender.ts)), which streams DDP over UDP (or HTTP when UDP does not get through), skips a frame the device already shows, and sends each device only as many frames as its strip can show (a 1,000-LED WS2812B output takes 30 ms per frame; the outputs and LED types are read from the device's settings, and outputs side by side count once). A device that no longer gets frames goes back to its own state at once; one that passes from one source to another just keeps going. The Play page's simulated yard goes to each phone 30 times a second, only when it changed, and compressed (deflate): mostly dark game frames shrink 5 to 25 times, so the phones leave the Wi-Fi to the lights. Games draw with the shared kit ([server/src/game/kit.ts](server/src/game/kit.ts)): the roof as seen from the street, sparks, waves, trails and motes that fly through the yard.

## Release

Push a tag such as `v2.0.0`. The workflow in [.github/workflows](.github/workflows) runs the checks and publishes an amd64 image to GHCR.
